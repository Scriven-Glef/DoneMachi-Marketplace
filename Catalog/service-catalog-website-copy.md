## PART 1 — Recommended Site Structure (Read This First)

**Don't create 90 individual product pages.** Use this structure instead:

```
Homepage
 ├── 3 Category Tiles: Plumbing | AC Repair | Electrical
 │
 ├── /plumbing  (1 page)
 │     Intro copy
 │     7 problem-groups, each expandable, each listing its jobs with price
 │
 ├── /ac-repair  (1 page)
 │     Intro copy
 │     8 problem-groups, expandable, jobs + price
 │
 └── /electrical (1 page)
       Intro copy
       9 problem-groups, expandable, jobs + price
```

That's **3 real pages** instead of 90+, and each one is content-rich enough to rank on Google for things like "AC repair in [City]" or "plumber near me."

**How to show pricing on each item:**
- Label every price as **"Starting from ₹X"**, not a flat price — final cost depends on the issue's severity, parts needed, etc. This protects you from disputes.
- Keep your low inspection/diagnosis fee (₹149–199) as the "walk in the door" price. Standard practice: **this fee is adjusted against the final bill** if the customer goes ahead with the repair. Say this explicitly — it removes the biggest objection people have to booking a home-service visit ("what if they just charge me for showing up?").

**Booking flow that matches this structure:**
1. Customer picks a category (Plumbing / AC / Electrical)
2. Selects their issue(s) from the grouped checklist (checkboxes, not 28 separate "Add to Cart" buttons)
3. Sees a running estimate total
4. Picks a time slot and confirms

**UI pattern:** each problem-group is a collapsed accordion row on the category page. Clicking it expands to show the individual jobs inside with their prices. This keeps the page short on load and lets people scan fast.
